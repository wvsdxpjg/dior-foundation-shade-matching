That's usually where the confusion starts. Dior doesn't sell one foundation with different labels — the Forever line is built around different finishes, different coverage levels and different skin types, and the names have shifted enough over the years that old reviews don't map cleanly onto what's on the shelf today. Add shade codes (0N, 1.5W, 4WO) and it gets worse.

Here's the practical version: what each Dior base actually is, who it suits, what it costs, and how to keep more of your money when you buy it.

### The size difference is bigger than the price difference

This is the part most people miss.

Dior Forever liquids come in **30 ml**. Backstage Face & Body comes in **50 ml** at roughly three-quarters the price. Dior's own UK product pages spell out the maths: Forever Skin Glow is listed at £1,800.00 per litre, Backstage at £820.00 per litre. That's the same brand, same counter, less than half the cost per millilitre.

If you're a daily wearer, that gap adds up fast. Backstage is a lighter, more natural-looking base than Forever Matte, so it isn't a straight swap for someone who wants high coverage. But if you're a skin-tint person who just wants evenness, paying Forever prices for a formula you'll use up in eight weeks is an expensive habit.

## Which one actually suits your skin

Cosmopolitan put the full Forever line through testing with three editors of different skin types, and the split came out roughly where you'd expect from the formulas.

**Dry or sensitive skin:** Forever Skin Glow. It carries the skincare-heavy ingredients — hyaluronic acid, niacinamide, peptides, glycerin, vitamin E — and Cosmo's dry-skinned editor described only needing two pumps to cover hyperpigmentation and redness, with the formula being non-comedogenic, which matters if you break out easily. The trade-off with glow formulas is shine control; set the T-zone if you're combination.

**Oily or acne-prone skin:** Forever Matte. The brand describes it as an 86% skincare base with wild pansy and rose extracts, and the finish is a smooth, shine-free natural matte. Cosmo's oily-skinned editor reported wearing it from 8am to about 6pm with barely a touch-up, which is a better real-world test than any "24-hour wear" number. Worth knowing: "24-hour makeup wear" is Dior's claim about the makeup, not about sun protection, and the SPF in these formulas is light — retailer pages list SPF20 for shades 0.5N to 4WO and SPF15 above that. Wear sunscreen underneath.

**Normal skin, on the go, or coverage only where you need it:** the Forever stick. Dior lists medium coverage and 24-hour wear and hydration. Cosmo crowned it in a stick-foundation category and its beauty director described swiping it around the nose, under the eyes and on the chin and blending with a brush. Sticks are the least fussy format and the easiest to top up.

**Face and body, humid weather, or the "second skin" look:** Backstage. It's the lightest and most natural of the four, and it's formulated to resist water and heat, which is why it gets used on shows. It claims a no-build-up finish, so expect a real skin effect rather than a masked one.

## Shade codes, decoded

Dior's naming looks cryptic, but it's consistent once you see the structure: a number for depth, a letter for undertone.

- **N** — neutral. Doesn't pull pink or yellow. The safest starting point if you've never been matched.
- **W** — warm. Golden or olive-leaning, built for skin that reads yellow or warm.
- **C** — cool. Pink-leaning, for cooler complexions.
- **WO / CR** and similar combos exist for skin that sits between two points — warm olive, cool rosy and so on.

The number is your depth on a roughly 0–9 scale (0 being the fairest, and half steps like 1.5 and 2.5 filling the gaps). So 1.5N is a light, neutral shade — the kind of mid-position that gets recommended often for light-to-light-medium skin tones.

The matching advice that keeps circulating — and that matches how the formulas behave — is to test on the jaw and drag the swatch down onto the neck, then walk outside and look in daylight. Counter lighting is designed to flatter everything and hide colour differences, and a shade that looks like a perfect match under a cool white counter light can look like a mask in natural light. Give it ten to fifteen minutes before you judge too, since a base settles as it warms up. Dior also runs a shade finder on its own site if you're ordering blind.

What buyers actually say: Sephora UK reviews for Forever Matte and Forever Skin Glow skew heavily to five stars, with recurring themes of a little going a long way, longevity through a working day, and a good shade range. The most useful negative review in that set came from a shopper who found the texture lovely but the coverage lighter than she wanted — a fair reminder that "high coverage" means different things to different people.

## FAQ

**Which Dior foundation is best for oily skin?**
Forever Matte. It's the matte counterpart to Skin Glow, with the same skincare-infused positioning but a shine-free set.

**Is Backstage good enough as a regular foundation?**
Yes, if you like lighter coverage. It's a 50 ml face-and-body base with a natural second-skin finish, so it covers unevenness rather than blemishes. It's also the cheapest Dior option per millilitre — £41 for 50 ml versus £54 for 30 ml on the Forever liquid in the UK.

**Do I need to change shade if Dior reformulates?**
Sephora's product pages state the shades didn't change in the most recent Forever refresh — "order your usual shade." Still worth a quick swatch if you're near a counter.

**Does Dior foundation replace sunscreen?**
No. Dior itself notes the 24-hour claim refers to makeup wear, not sun protection, and the SPF built into these formulas is modest. Layer your usual sunscreen underneath.

**Can I claim cashback and still use a Dior gift-with-purchase?**
A gift-with-purchase is different from redeeming a voucher, so it usually doesn't void a session — but gift card purchases and redemptions generally do. Read the specific retailer's exclusion list rather than assuming.
